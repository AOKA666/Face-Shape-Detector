# Face Shape Types Explained

If you are unsure whether your face is oval, round, square, heart, diamond, or oblong, this guide breaks each type down with practical visual checks.

Face shape is not about perfection. It is about proportions: width, length, forehead line, cheekbone prominence, and jawline angle.

## Why Face Shape Types Matter

Knowing your face shape helps you choose:

- Haircuts with better balance
- Glasses that complement your structure
- Beard and grooming direction
- Makeup placement that works with your natural lines

If you want objective confirmation first, run the [face shape detector online](/face-shape-detector-online) or [detect your face shape from a photo](/face-shape-detector-from-photo).

## Oval Face Shape

Typical markers:

- Face length is slightly greater than width
- Forehead is a little wider than jawline
- Jawline is rounded, not sharp
- Overall proportions look balanced

Oval faces usually adapt well to many styles with minimal correction.

## Round Face Shape

Typical markers:

- Width and length are close
- Cheek area appears fullest
- Jawline is soft
- Few strong angular lines

Round faces usually benefit from cuts and frames that add structure.

## Square Face Shape

Typical markers:

- Forehead, cheekbones, and jawline are close in width
- Jawline looks broad and angular
- Face appears structured rather than tapered

Square faces often look best with styles that add movement or soften hard lines.

## Heart Face Shape

Typical markers:

- Forehead is wider than jawline
- Face narrows toward the chin
- Chin appears narrower or pointed

Heart shapes often benefit from styling that adds visual weight to the lower face.

## Diamond Face Shape

Typical markers:

- Cheekbones are the widest area
- Forehead is narrower
- Jawline is narrower
- Chin may appear pointed

Diamond faces usually balance well with soft framing near forehead and jaw.

## Oblong Face Shape

Typical markers:

- Face length is clearly greater than width
- Side lines appear straighter and longer
- Forehead, cheeks, and jawline can be similar in width

Oblong faces generally need width and reduced vertical emphasis.

## Fast Self-Check Flow

1. Pull hair back and use a front-facing photo
2. Compare face length versus width
3. Identify your widest area: forehead, cheekbones, or jawline
4. Check whether jawline is soft, angular, or narrow
5. Match the closest category, then validate with AI
