# How to Tell Your Face Shape From a Selfie

Your face shape affects which hairstyles, glasses, beard styles, and makeup techniques look most balanced on you. The problem is that most people guess wrong.

The easiest way to figure it out is to use a clear selfie and check a few visible proportions: forehead width, cheekbone width, jawline width, and face length.

## What You Need

- A front-facing selfie
- Good natural light
- Hair pulled away from the face
- Neutral expression
- A mirror or photo editor for reference

If your hair covers your forehead or jawline, your guess will probably be off.

## Step 1: Take the Right Selfie

A usable selfie should have:

- Your full face visible
- Camera at eye level
- No heavy angle from above or below
- No strong smile that changes cheek shape
- No filters

Bad input gives bad classification. If the photo is tilted, zoomed too close, or half-covered by hair, the result gets messy fast.

## Step 2: Look at the Widest Part of Your Face

Start with one simple question:

**Which area looks widest?**

- **Forehead widest** → often heart shape
- **Cheekbones widest** → often diamond or oval
- **Jawline widest** → often square or pear-type patterns
- **All similar widths** → often oval, round, or square

This one step eliminates a lot of wrong guesses.

## Step 3: Compare Face Length vs Face Width

Now compare the vertical length of your face with the width across the cheekbones.

- **Longer than wide** → oval or oblong is more likely
- **Nearly equal length and width** → round or square is more likely

If your face clearly looks longer than it is wide, stop trying to force it into round.

## Step 4: Check Your Jawline Shape

Your jawline is one of the strongest signals.

- **Soft, curved jawline** → round or oval
- **Sharp, angular jawline** → square
- **Narrow chin** → heart or diamond
- **Longer, straighter sides** → oblong

A lot of people only look at the chin. That is not enough. Look at the whole lower face.

## Step 5: Match Your Features to Common Face Shapes

## Oval Face Shape

Typical signs:

- Face length is greater than width
- Forehead slightly wider than jawline
- Jawline is soft, not sharp
- Overall proportions look balanced

Oval is the most flexible shape for hair, glasses, and styling.

## Round Face Shape

Typical signs:

- Width and length are similar
- Full cheeks
- Soft jawline
- Few sharp angles

Round faces usually benefit from styles that add height and visual structure.

## Square Face Shape

Typical signs:

- Forehead, cheekbones, and jawline are similar in width
- Jawline looks strong and angular
- Face length and width are relatively close

Square faces usually suit styles that soften or frame the jaw.

## Heart Face Shape

Typical signs:

- Forehead wider than jawline
- Cheekbones can be prominent
- Chin is narrow or pointed

Heart-shaped faces often suit styles that add balance around the lower face.

## Diamond Face Shape

Typical signs:

- Cheekbones are the widest point
- Forehead is narrower
- Jawline is narrower
- Chin may look more pointed

Diamond faces often benefit from styles that add width to the forehead or jaw area.

## Oblong Face Shape

Typical signs:

- Face is noticeably longer than wide
- Sides of the face look straighter
- Forehead, cheeks, and jawline may be fairly similar

Oblong faces usually look better with styles that add width rather than height.

## Common Mistakes People Make

### Mistake 1: Letting Hair Decide the Result

Bangs, volume, and side parts change how your face looks. Always judge the bone structure, not the haircut.

### Mistake 2: Using an Angled Selfie

A slightly tilted selfie can make the jawline look sharper or the forehead look wider than it really is.

### Mistake 3: Confusing Round and Oval

If your face is clearly longer than it is wide, it is probably not round.

### Mistake 4: Over-focusing on One Feature

You are not your chin alone. Face shape comes from proportion, not one isolated detail.
