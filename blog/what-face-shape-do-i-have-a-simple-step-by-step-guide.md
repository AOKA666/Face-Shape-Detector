# What Face Shape Do I Have? A Simple Step-by-Step Guide

If you have ever wondered, **what face shape do I have**, you are not alone. It is one of the most common beauty and styling questions online, and for good reason. Your face shape influences which haircuts, glasses, brows, beard styles, and makeup techniques look most flattering.

The good news: you do not need to overcomplicate it.

This guide walks you through a simple way to identify your face shape step by step.

## Why Face Shape Matters

Knowing your face shape helps you make better styling decisions in areas like:

- Hairstyles
- Glasses and sunglasses
- Beard styles
- Brow shaping
- Makeup and contour placement
- Photo posing and framing

It is not a rulebook. It is a shortcut to what usually looks more balanced.

## Step 1: Start With a Clear Front View

You can use:

- A mirror
- A front-facing selfie
- A portrait photo with your hair pulled back

For the best result:

- Stand in even lighting
- Keep your head straight
- Use a neutral expression
- Make sure your forehead and jawline are visible

If your hair covers the outline of your face, fix that first.

## Step 2: Look at These 4 Areas

When identifying face shape, focus on these proportions:

1. **Forehead width**
2. **Cheekbone width**
3. **Jawline width**
4. **Face length**

You do not need exact millimeter precision, but you do need honesty.

## Step 3: Ask These Questions

### Is Your Face Longer Than It Is Wide?

- **Yes** → oval or oblong may be likely
- **No, they are pretty close** → round or square may be more likely

### Which Part Looks Widest?

- **Forehead** → heart shape
- **Cheekbones** → diamond or oval
- **Jawline** → square or lower-heavy face shape
- **All fairly similar** → round, square, or oval

### Is Your Jawline Soft or Angular?

- **Soft and curved** → round or oval
- **Sharp and defined** → square
- **Narrow and pointed** → heart or diamond

## Step 4: Match Yourself to the Main Face Shapes

## Oval

You may have an oval face if:

- Your face is longer than it is wide
- Your forehead is slightly wider than your jaw
- Your jawline is rounded
- Your features look balanced overall

Oval faces can usually wear the widest range of styles.

## Round

You may have a round face if:

- Your face length and width are similar
- Your cheeks look full
- Your jawline is soft
- Your face has few strong angles

Round faces often suit styles that create height or definition.

## Square

You may have a square face if:

- Your forehead, cheekbones, and jawline are close in width
- Your jawline looks strong and angular
- Your face is broad rather than long

Square faces often benefit from styles that soften edges or create movement.

## Heart

You may have a heart-shaped face if:

- Your forehead is wider than your jawline
- Your chin is narrow
- Your face tapers downward

Heart-shaped faces often suit styles that add balance around the chin and jaw area.

## Diamond

You may have a diamond face if:

- Your cheekbones are the widest point
- Your forehead is narrower
- Your jawline is narrower
- Your chin may look pointed

Diamond faces often work well with styles that add softness or width in the upper or lower face.

## Oblong

You may have an oblong face if:

- Your face is clearly longer than it is wide
- Your forehead, cheeks, and jawline are relatively even
- Your face sides appear straighter

Oblong faces often benefit from styles that reduce the visual effect of length.

## Step 5: Double-Check With a Photo

If you are still unsure, compare your mirror impression with a straight-on photo. A photo makes proportion easier to judge because you are not constantly moving or changing angle.

You can also use an AI face shape detector to confirm your guess.

## When People Get It Wrong

Most mistakes happen for one of these reasons:

- Hair covers the true outline of the face
- The selfie angle distorts width and jawline
- People confuse round with oval
- People focus too much on the chin only
- Weight fluctuation changes softness, but not underlying structure
